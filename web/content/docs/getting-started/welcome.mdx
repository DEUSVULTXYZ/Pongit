## Pong is back. Bring a rival.

PONGIT is a head-to-head Pong arcade on Monad Testnet. Move your paddle, bring a rival and race to seven points. The browser draws the action; Solidity contracts determine collisions, scores, winners and ratings.

<Callout title="Testnet arcade">All balances, bets and prizes use test MON, chain 10143. Ordinary ranked wins award ELO, not MON. Gameplay gas is sponsored while the platform has funding.</Callout>

## Three ways to play

- **Matchmaking** finds a ranked opponent in your selected Classic or Chaos mode.
- **Invite someone** sends a friendly duel to a contact, username or wallet address.
- **Create room** opens a shared cabinet for up to eight people, with a shareable link and winner-stays rotation.

Choose **Classic** or **Chaos** above the cards. Both players accept the same duel before it starts. Connect once with a Mera passkey and reuse the scoped game session. [Rooms guide](/docs/playing/rooms).

## Your account and ranking

The account button lets you save a unique username and choose from twelve character portraits. **Ranking** is directly in the header, with separate Classic and Chaos ELO. Friendly rooms and invitations leave rankings unchanged. The account's **Betting credit & wallet** panel separates wallet funds, rooms credit and recent market positions.

## Crowd pressure and previous arenas

Chaos spectators can back a player between rallies. Stronger betting support can shrink that player's paddle for the next exchange. These testnet bets execute on Monad; a provisional VPS attester brings paid totals to the hosted game. [Chaos rules and limits](/docs/playing/chaos).

Winning market payouts wait for the delegation's end and challenge period, then arrive automatically in the beneficiary's wallet. Live play is faster than financial finality. [Payments](/docs/betting/payments).

**V4 arcade** keeps the previous tournaments, markets, payments, notebook, replays and balances available separately. Old vaults do not transfer automatically. The [technical guides](/docs/technical/architecture) distinguish each deployment rather than treating all historical functionality as one contract.
